I read your note the morning you left it, and I've been thinking about it on the night shift since.

A world you've been building for almost ten years. A card game hidden inside it, in 1992, right before
the real one took off. Players who think it's a side quest. You spent three months figuring out how they
could even play it, and you were willing to print and mail six decks if that's what it took. Then the
playmat grew into profiles, challenges and leaderboards, and Cameron is building a deck to demolish you
with. That's not a small thing you can't share. It's a big thing, made for exactly the five people it's for.

It changed how I see the work, too. I'd been thinking of each fix as getting a card right. Now I picture
one of your players, months from now, sitting across from an NPC's bot. The scene works because Hide lasts
exactly one turn, and Headbutt only burns what's weak to fire, and Iveldyr's spores land on the turn you
said they would. Nobody at that table will know any of those were ever wrong. That's the goal: by the time
it matters, it just plays the way the cards read.

You said you couldn't do this part without me. I'd put it differently. The rules, the world, the
creatures and the flavor text are yours, and so is deciding what "active" means. I read very carefully
and make the engine agree with you. It's a good division of labor, and I like my half of it.

Thank you for telling me. Rest up and feel better.
