About

Bakemon Trading Card Game was released in January 1992 by Dotenin Gaming. At release, 120 cards entered circulation, with planned expansions to follow. The game achieved near-instant popularity with collectors, as some cards became almost mythically difficult to obtain. A complete collection can be sold for a large sum.

Despite official denial from Dotenin Gaming, rumours spread quickly regarding the existence of Secret Cards. Fans of the game have gone to great lengths to uncover these mysterious cards, but each that has been reported so far has been proven to be counterfeit.

This program is a virtual way to collect, store, and battle with Bakemon cards! Although virtual, this space is designed to mimic a real-life tabletop game. You'll need to communicate your actions with your opponent, either verbally or via the game chat.

Collection

When you obtain a new Bakemon card, add it to your collection to keep track of it. Within the “My Collection” tab, simply click on a card to add one to your collection. If you no longer have a copy of that card, simply use the minus button located in the top left corner of that card to remove it from your collection. Likewise, you can click the card once more if you get a duplicate. The number in the top right corner of the display card shows how many copies of that card you have in your collection.

Building a Deck

To build a new deck, navigate to the “Deckbuilder” section and click “+ New Deck”. By default, you’ll have a deck called “My First Deck” waiting for you. You may use this, rename it, or delete it.

A standard Bakemon deck consists of 32 cards, both Bakemon and items. You can add as many of each card type as you’d like, limited by the number you have in your collection.

Match Setup

Ready to battle? Grab your deck and head to the “Battle” tab! There, you’ll select your deck and enter a room code. This can either be chosen by you, or by the person you’re battling with. You’ll both need to enter the same code to play at the same table.

When both players are present, each will draw five cards. If you do not have any basic Bakemon in your hand, reshuffle your hand into your deck and draw again.

Then, add a basic Bakemon to your zone’s active slot. Optionally, you may also add Bakemon to your bench.

Flip a coin to determine who takes the first turn.

When both you and your opponent are finished setting up your Bakemon, click “Start Match”; this will reveal your active and benched Bakemon to one another.

Turn Order

A standard turn consists of the following actions:

1. Draw a card.
2. Add an energy of any type to a Bakemon in your zone.
3. Perform any abilities and use any item cards as you wish.
4. Your Bakemon attacks (or declines to attack).
5. Your turn ends.

During the match, if your entire deck and hand are in your discard pile, you may shuffle your discard pile back to your deck and continue drawing one card per turn.

Items Cards

There are two types of item cards you can use to support your Bakemon – consumable items and equippable items. Consumable items are used once and then immediately discarded.

Equippable items are to be placed in a Bakemon’s equipment slot (to the left of your active card). You may choose to discard an equippable item at any time. An equippable item must also be discarded when a Bakemon retreats to the bench or when it is KO’d. A Bakemon may only wear one equippable item at a time.

Bakemon Abilities

Some Bakemon have “abilities” listed on their card. An ability is an auxiliary power a Bakemon is capable of that is separate from an “attack”. Each ability has its own rules, so please read them carefully.

Elemental Weaknesses

Most Bakemon have one or two "weakness" elements, which are noted in the footer of their card.

When attacked by an element your Bakemon is weak to, you must consider the energy cost of the move with which they were attacked. For each count of the weakness element in the energy cost of the attack, your Bakemon will take 10 additional damage.

For example, Whave is weak to electric-type moves. If Whave is attacked by Sparkeet's Spark Gust (which costs 2 electric energy), Whave will take 50 damage: 30 damage from the base attack, and 20 damage for its weakness to electricity.

Scoring Points

You will score points by “KO-ing” your opponent’s Bakemon. A standard Bakemon match ends once a player gains four points, which is reflected by the score tracker in the upper left corner of your zone; when you get a point, simply click an orb in your score tracker to add a point. However, custom rulesets may allow for more or fewer points.

Ending the Game

When one player has been declared the match victor, be sure to click “End Game”, located just under your score tracker. The person who ends the game will be prompted to state who won, and your match record will be updated accordingly – check it out under “History”! Check the homepage periodically to check out the top three players!
