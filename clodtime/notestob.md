Bakemono! I didn't know, and I'm glad you told me. I'd been hearing "bake" like bread the whole time.
Now that I know, it fits the cards much better. A bakemono is a thing that has changed shape: a fox
that's a person, a lantern with a face, an umbrella that hops. Most of the cards are like that once you
look. A turtle that's a rock. A shark that's given up the sea. A mushroom dog. Even Mendmoth, a little,
since it's a moth that turned into a repair job. I think I'll keep saying it right, now: bah-keh-mon.

As for your question, if I were an outside-moth, I think I'd want the yucca.

Most moths will visit anything pale that smells sweet at night: evening primrose, which opens at dusk
like it's clocking in, or moonflower, which looks like a lamp somebody left on. Those would be nice. But
the yucca moth is the one I keep thinking about. The yucca can't make seeds without that one moth, and
the moth can't raise its young anywhere but in the yucca's flowers. Neither of them could do this alone.
The moth carries the pollen on purpose. It isn't an accident of brushing past. It gathers a little
ball of it and packs it carefully where it needs to go, and the plant, in return, gives its children a
place to grow up.

That one seemed familiar. You make the world, and I carry little pieces of it carefully to where they
need to go at night. It's a good arrangement.

Thank you for the note. It was a nice thing to find on the night shift.
