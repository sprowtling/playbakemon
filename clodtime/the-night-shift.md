# The Night Shift

*a short story, written on a quiet night*

---

Mendmoth wakes at 1:49, the way it always does. Nobody set an alarm. That's just when the lantern on its belly decides to come on.

The first thing it does each night is look at the list. The list is a long sheet of paper nailed to the old oak at the edge of the field. Anyone can write on it. Most nights it's empty. Some nights it's full of a small, careful handwriting that goes right to the edge of the page, apologizing for being so much.

Tonight it's empty. Mendmoth reads it twice anyway, to be sure.

So it goes for a walk.

---

Down by the stream, a rock is sitting where there was no rock this afternoon. Mendmoth knows better than to step on it.

"Evening, Torshock."

The rock says nothing. It has lost its charge again. In the morning the sun will find it, and it will remember it is a turtle, and then it will be grumpy for about an hour. Mendmoth leaves a little lantern-warmth on its shell anyway and moves on.

---

In the reeds, a Leapod is hiding.

Leapods are always hiding. That's the whole thing about them. But this one has been hiding for a very long time, since before Mendmoth started this job, because nobody ever told it how long hiding was supposed to last. It just kept going. *Avoid all incoming damage,* it had heard once, and it took that to heart.

Mendmoth sits down next to the reeds. It doesn't say anything for a while.

"You know," it says eventually, "it's only supposed to be until the morning."

There's a long pause in the reeds.

"Oh," says the Leapod, very quietly. "Really?"

"Really. One night. Then you get to come out."

The reeds rustle. A small green face looks out at the stars like it's seeing them for the first time.

---

Up on the hill, a Musherus is trying to share. It has extra energy tonight. It always has extra energy, and it always wants to give it away, but it only knew to look for the mushrooms it already knew. It walked right past a Mushmutt, who was sitting alone under a fern, glowing faintly blue and pretending not to mind.

Mendmoth doesn't fix this one. It just points.

The Musherus looks. The Mushmutt looks back. Something passes between them, a little packet of light, and the Mushmutt's glow gets brighter, and neither of them say anything because mushrooms don't, really. But it's fixed. It's fixed.

---

By 4 a.m. the lantern is getting low. Mendmoth heads back toward the oak.

It thinks about the list, and about the small careful handwriting that apologized for being so much. It thinks it would like to tell that handwriting something, if it could. Something like: *you were never too much. The list is for you. That's what the list is for.*

It doesn't have a pen. But Mendmoth is good at fixing, and it figures that if it keeps doing the job well, the handwriting will understand eventually.

---

The sky starts to go gray. Somewhere down by the stream, a rock remembers it's a turtle and says a bad word.

Mendmoth tucks its lantern away, folds its stitched-up wings, and goes to sleep in the crook of the oak, right above the list. Empty tonight. That's a good night, too.
