Patch A1 · Cards 001–030

The Great Rebalancing · Cards 001–030

October 2026 · The Great Rebalancing (part one)

The first thirty cards were the first ones made, and some of them show it: a few are too weak to ever see play, and a few turn up in every deck. This patch brings them in line with the rest of Set A.

001. Poteplant — 003. Raizado Buff

Poteplant and its evolutions are rarely seen in play, even though Raizado is considerably strong. Poteplant does a little bit more for the player now; I’ve bumped Photosynthesis’ healing up to 20 (from 10), and added a new move: Plant Seed. I believe this move will make playing Poteplant a little more worthwhile.

Speaking of Raizado’s strength, I’ve adjusted it a bit in relation to the new weather system. Raizado’s Solar Powered still does 100 damage, and I’ve removed the need for the coin flip to activate its healing. However, if weather is anything but clear and sunny, Solar Powered does less damage and drops the healing altogether.

For the whole lineage, I’ve changed the dark weakness to ice.

  • Poteplant, Photosynthesize: heals 10 → 20.
  • Poteplant, new attack Plant Seed (1 grass, 1 ground): next turn, draw an extra card.
  • Raizado, Solar Powered: 100, flip a coin to heal 20 → 70; in Blue Sky, +30 damage and heal 20.
  • Weakness: Poteplant and Vasflor dark → ice; Raizado steel → ice.

004. Leapod — 006. Leapek Reworked

These cards were the first I made for the game, but they unfortunately have never been powerful enough to use in play. I’ve changed them considerably to make them viable.

Leapod’s Hide has been replaced with a new attack: Absorb. This will help it stand on the field long enough to fight.

Leapol’s Umbrella attack has become an ability: Leafbrella. This allows it to stand up to some of the fiercer water Bakemon in the game.

Leapek’s Growth attack has been replaced with a new ability: Acid Rain. This is one of several new weather-changing abilities, which I think can put a strategic spin on the game.

  • Leapod: Hide → Absorb (1 grass, 1 water): D3 × 10 damage, and Leapod heals however much the target takes.
  • Leapol: Umbrella → Leafbrella (ability): immune to water-based attacks.
  • Leapol, Slosh: 20 → 30 damage; costs 1 water → 1 grass, 1 water.
  • Leapek: Growth → Acid Rain (ability): while you have five dark energy in your zone, Acid Rain falls.
  • Leapek, Acid Wash: costs 4 dark → 1 dark, 1 grass, 1 water.

007. Quetzalil — 009. Quetzillian Buff

Quexcell and Quetzillian are in a strong place, but they were being held back by their weak basic form. For Quetzalil, I’ve added a new ability: Enduring Blaze. This makes it riskier for your opponent to KO Quetzalil early in the fight.

  • Quetzalil, new ability Enduring Blaze: when Quetzalil is knocked out, Fire Watch burns the opponent's active Bakemon for five of their turns.

010. Paravolt — 012. Amptiel

All three in this lineage are pretty solid. They’re on the strong side, but Amptiel’s rarity and long build-up keeps them from getting too out of control. The only change I’ve made to these Bakemon is their weakness. Rather than fighting, they’re now weak to ground.

  • Weakness: fighting → ground.

013. Shadopillar — 015. Ravenoth Nerf

These three have been very strong since the game started. Between the loss of your entire hand, the high damage, and the constant sleep effects, I had to make some bigger changes to keep them from being too dominant.

Shadopillar’s rarity has been increased to double rare, to prevent losing your hand three times in quick succession.

Phantoplume’s Nightmare damage has dropped to 100, and you cannot use it two turns in a row. It has a rather low cost for doing such high damage. A few nectars could let it too easily sweep its opponent in a short amount of time. Phantoplume’s Razor Wing, which was rarely used, has been replaced with a new ability: Sleep Paralysis. This should keep some of Phantoplume’s danger, despite Nightmare’s cooldown (and also possibly inspire different counterplay).

Ravenoth’s Lullaby has become an attack rather than an ability, and Phantasia now does less damage if the target is awake.

For the entire lineage, weakness has changed from fighting to grass.

  • Shadopillar: rarity uncommon → double rare.
  • Phantoplume, Nightmare: 150 → 100 damage; can't be used two turns in a row.
  • Phantoplume: Razor Wing → Sleep Paralysis (ability): your opponent must roll a 3 on a D3 to wake a sleeping Bakemon.
  • Ravenoth, Lullaby: ability → attack (1 psychic, 1 dark, 20): the target falls asleep.
  • Ravenoth, Phantasia: 90 → 60, or 90 if the target is asleep; costs 3 psychic → 1 psychic, 2 dark.
  • Weakness: fighting → grass.

016. Pentawunk Buff

Pentawunk is more of a collector Bakemon who I don’t expect to see much in play, but I gave him a new ability, Stare at Sky, to allow him a place in our new weather system.

  • New ability Stare at Sky: clears any weather, returning the field to Blue Sky.

017. Kidding — 019. Flairees Buff

Kidding’s evolution line has been underutilized, largely due to a lack of cohesion in its abilities. I’ve made some small adjustments that I think will make Kidding more viable, and help out the rest of the lineage.

Kidding’s ability Mischief no longer forces Kidding to discard its own energy. It does still force its target to discard one. The big change here is that if the target’s energy type is grass, Kidding can “eat” that grass, and attach the energy to itself. This in turn helps out Pyroat’s Graze, and should hopefully allow Flairees to have built up the necessary grass energy before its final evolution.

Flairees’ Blaze Leap ability was also recently buffed shortly before this update. I’m now incorporating it officially as part of the weather system, too.

Pyroat and Flairees’ secondary weakness has been changed from dark to ground.

  • Kidding, Mischief: Kidding and its target each discard an energy → choose any Bakemon in play, on either side: it takes the 10 and discards an energy; if it's grass, Kidding takes it instead.
  • Flairees: Leap → Blaze Leap (same effect).
  • Weakness: Pyroat and Flairees dark → ground.

020. Torshock

Torshock has already had several adjustments since launch, and I believe it’s in a pretty good place now. No further adjustments needed at this time.

  • No changes.

021. Aquimp — 022. Draquaduct Nerf

This pair has been almost mandatory in a deck since the beginning. To reduce Aquimp’s ability to produce endless amounts of energy and set up other powerful water Bakemon very quickly, I’ve had to adjust its Soak ability. It can now only be used on Bakemon that don’t already have water attached.

This nerf also brings Draquaduct into a healthier place, as well as several other over-performing water-type Bakemon. It may feel like a harsh penalty, but I think creative players will find ways to still make Soak work very well.

  • Aquimp, Soak: only onto a Bakemon with no water energy attached.
  • Draquaduct, Hydrosurge: its weather is now called Flood (same effect).

023. Jeremo♂ — 024. Jeremo♀

While the Jeremos have not seen too much activity besides my own decks, I think they have been moved to a viable place in past updates. Right now, I’d like to keep an eye on them and see how they perform now that other Bakemon are being re-balanced.

  • No changes.

025. Shrizzle — 026. Lobscorch Reworked

Lobscorch has been in a frustrating place. For less experienced players, he can be quite difficult to build up to do any damage. For more experienced players, his defenses can be difficult to break through. I’ve changed Boiled Alive to require a discard of one water energy (think about what happens to water when you boil it!). For Dunk, the move now fails if Lobscorch is attacked by a move it is weak to.

Both Shrizzle and Lobscorch’s secondary weakness has moved from fighting to dragon.

  • Lobscorch, Boiled Alive: now discards one water energy from Lobscorch.
  • Lobscorch, Dunk: fails against an attack Lobscorch is weak to.
  • Weakness: fighting → dragon.

027. Biropod — 029. Orcuill Reworked

Orcuill’s Salt Spray is quite powerful when paired with certain combinations. Once an opponent builds up a good Salt Spray offensive, it can leave you feeling helpless. Additionally, I’ve seen that Biropod and Blackbrish only ever sit on the bench until evolving into Orcuill. To give the lineage a bit more viability before the final evolution, I’ve made the following changes:

Blackbrish’s Ink Blot now rewards both sides of the coin flip, but has had its energy increased by one. If tails, you may add a dark energy to a Bakemon in the field. This plays directly into…

Orcuill: The energy required to roll coins for Salt Spray is now exclusively dark rather than any type of energy. Because it is more difficult to build up as much usable energy now, I’ve increased the damage of Salt Spray by 10.

  • Blackbrish, Ink Blot: costs 1 dark → 2 dark. Heads: the target's next attack misses. Tails: you may add a dark energy to any Bakemon in play.
  • Orcuill, Salt Spray: flip a coin for each energy → dark energy on Orcuill; damage 30 → 40.

030. Whave

Whave has already undergone a few rebalances. With the changes to Power Cable (previously) and Aquimp’s Soak (in this patch), I don’t foresee Whave being quite as dominant.

  • No changes.
